Architecture

Four controllers, one canvas, three numbers the venue actually buys

Dwell, spend per head, and return rate. Everything below exists to move one of those three, and every cue in the library is traceable to one of them. The split between the four controllers is not organisational tidiness — each one owns a different scarce resource, and they fail in different ways.

The three layers of dead time

One monitor per broadcast · certainty rises with the layer

The layers are not just categories, they are different certainty regimes. L3 is known days in advance and can be sold before the week starts. L2 is signalled by the game state and can be forecast minutes out — remaining_timeouts tells you a stoppage is coming before it arrives. L1 is detected at the moment it starts and its length is never known, so only its minimum is ever trusted. Longest and most certain at the top, shortest and least certain at the bottom — and the room is switched away from a broadcast only during that broadcast\u2019s own dead time.

Sold inventory

Seven units · brand creative shown is placeholder

Every unit above runs on outputs the box controls, inside dead time the box detected, and only after switching away from the broadcast. No feed is ever modified and nothing is ever laid over live gameplay — which is the whole reason this inventory is sellable at all. Sources carry a profile and the policy engine decides per source which of the five switch windows are open, so a restricted source simply has fewer slots rather than a different product.
